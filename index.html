02 / The incident

The fraud arrived inside a real conversation.

01 · ACCESS

A mailbox was compromised

An attacker gained unauthorised access to a company email account.

02 · DECEPTION

Payment instructions changed

The attacker replied in an existing customer thread and introduced alternative bank details.

03 · CONCEALMENT

Replies were hidden

A mailbox rule concealed correspondence from the affected customer.

04 · PAYMENT

The customer acted

A substantial payment for genuine invoices went to the fraudulent account.

This was business email compromise: a legitimate mailbox was used for a fraudulent business instruction.
The documents establish unauthorised access. They do not establish the original access method. Do not claim that a phishing link was clicked, a password was shared, or MFA was absent.
03 / What the investigation found

The funds were recovered.
The weakness still mattered.

Confirmed in the later assessment

A telephone conversation exposed the deception. The customer subsequently recovered the transferred funds; no final financial loss resulted from the fraudulent payment.

Scope of the account review

No evidence of further fraudulent messages or suspicious access to other data was found. The tenant-wide review had identified no comparable suspicious sign-ins in other accounts.

No safety-relevant maintenance information was available through the affected account. The investigation did not identify a direct maintenance safety impact.

“No evidence found” describes the review result. It does not guarantee that every possible activity was ruled out.

The first assessment still described recovery as uncertain. The later assessment records recovery, so it supplies the outcome used here. Wider organisational consequences remain relevant despite that recovery.
04 / Why there were two assessments

Protect the account.
Protect the decision.

Information security · ISMS

Can someone misuse our account or information?

Examines access, authenticity of communications, information exposure, financial exposure and reputation.

Safety management interface · SMS

What happens when people act on a false instruction?

Examines verification barriers and wider organisational effects: financial loss, unpaid receivables, disputes and business disruption.

Technical protection and independent business checks need to work together.
Explain ISMS as the information security management system, SMS as the safety management system. The second assessment addresses organisational risks through the interface; it does not establish that this incident caused an aircraft safety event.
05 / A simplified barrier view

Where could the chain be interrupted?

Threats & conditions

Access + convincing context

A compromised account, a genuine customer thread, a plausible business explanation and changed payment details.

Prevent before action

Strengthen authentication. Review mailbox rules. Independently verify changed payment instructions through an established channel.

Undesirable event

A fraudulent instruction is accepted as authentic and acted upon.

The sender and conversation look familiar. The changed instruction is not independently verified.

Recovery & consequences

Detect, report, respond

In this case, a telephone call exposed the fraud and the customer recovered the funds.

What could follow?

Financial loss, unpaid receivables, disputes, reputational harm or disruption of normal business.

Teaching illustration derived from the assessments. Preventive measures include proposed actions; they are not presented as verified completed controls.

A successful recovery is a useful recovery barrier, but not a reliable substitute for prevention. Do not imply that money can always be recovered. This teaching view is not a replacement for the formal risk records.

07 / Actions

How we addressed the risks.

Strengthen account protection

Review authentication, multi-factor authentication (MFA), device registration and access controls.

Check mailbox settings

Company-wide review of mailbox rules and forwarding for unexplained changes.

Verify payment changes

Assess a formal process for independently verifying changed bank details through an established contact channel.

Improve risk assessments

Revise the assessment form to show preventive and recovery barriers, including missing or ineffective checks.

These are the treatment actions defined in the two assessments. Completion has not been independently confirmed. In particular, the second assessment calls for assessing a formal verification process and revising the risk assessment form.
08 / Training & lessons learned · About 10 minutes

Recognise the change,
even when the sender looks right.

!

Different payment details

An unexpected bank-account change needs independent verification.

?

A surprising explanation

Tax issues, an unfamiliar subsidiary or unusual wording may be reasons to pause.

↗

A new payment destination

A destination inconsistent with the established arrangement deserves a check.

A familiar address and a genuine email thread do not authenticate a new instruction.

A foreign IBAN is not proof of fraud. The warning is the unexplained change and lack of independent verification.

The assessments identify unusual wording, an alleged subsidiary, changed bank details and a foreign IBAN as warning signs. Teach the combination and the deviation from established arrangements, not nationality as a fraud test.
09 / Discussion exercise · Fictional example

What would you do before acting?

From: a familiar business contact · In an existing invoice conversation

“Due to a temporary tax issue, our subsidiary will receive this payment. Please use the new bank details below for the outstanding invoices.”

Illustrative wording only. No real address, account number or original email is reproduced.

Identify two reasons to pause. Then describe how you would verify the request.

Reveal the discussion points
  • The payment details changed unexpectedly.
  • The explanation introduces an unfamiliar subsidiary and a tax issue.
  • Pause the payment or the forwarding of the instruction.
  • Contact the business partner using a number already held in trusted records—not a new number in this message.
  • Escalate discrepancies through the company’s authorised reporting route.
Ask the group before revealing. A reply to the same email is not independent verification if the mailbox is compromised. Adapt the response to the organisation's approved payment procedures.
10 / A practical response

Pause. Verify. Report.

01

Pause the action

Do not act on an unexplained changed instruction while its authenticity remains unresolved.

02

Verify independently

Use an established contact channel from trusted records. Confirm both the requested change and who authorised it.

03

Report promptly

Notify the designated company contact. Preserve the message and explain what has already happened.

If a payment has already gone out: escalate immediately so the authorised finance team can contact the bank and coordinate the response.

Training guidance. Apply the company’s approved procedures and reporting arrangements.

Confirm the reporting contact before delivery. Do not ask ordinary staff to investigate account logs or negotiate with an attacker. Reporting should be prompt even where an employee is unsure or has already acted.
11 / Discussion exercise · Fictional example

Your inbox is unusually quiet.

You find an unfamiliar rule that moves one customer’s replies out of your inbox. You did not create it.

What do you report—and what should you avoid doing on your own?
Reveal the discussion points
  • Report the unfamiliar rule and missing correspondence through the designated route.
  • Note what you observed and when. Retain the relevant messages.
  • Follow IT or incident-response instructions before changing or deleting suspicious configurations.
  • Do not contact a suspected attacker or attempt your own investigation.
  • Do not assume the issue is harmless because the account still works normally.

The case included a rule that concealed customer replies. This exercise is illustrative.

A mailbox-review action was defined in the information security assessment. IT should provide platform-specific instructions and preserve relevant evidence. This presentation deliberately avoids inventing the organisation's helpdesk process.
12 / The lessons

Make the check easier
than the mistake.

Authentic account ≠ authentic instruction

Independent checks protect decisions even when a real account has been misused.

Recovery ≠ prevention

The funds were recovered in this case. A future payment may not be.

People need a clear route

Make it easy to raise a concern and pause an unusual request without blame.

Controls need evidence

Defining an action is only the start. Management must confirm it is implemented and works.

These lessons are a teaching synthesis. Separate organisational accountability for clear procedures from staff accountability for using them. Avoid characterising the incident as simply a careless employee.
13 / Your take-away

If the instruction changes,
check it independently.

Pause the action. Use a trusted contact channel. Report a concern early—even if you are unsure or have already acted.

Discussion: where would you find a trusted number, and who would you contact in our organisation?

Facilitator: confirm the approved reporting route and verification procedure with staff before closing the session.

Ask participants to name their real reporting route. If it is unclear, resolve that before using the material as operational guidance. Attendance or exercise responses are not recorded by this website.
